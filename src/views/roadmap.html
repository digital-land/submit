Making it easier for councils to provide planning data

The Ministry of Housing, Communities and Local Government (MHCLG) is improving the Check and provide your planning data service. Local planning authorities (LPAs) use it to check their data against national standards and provide it to planning.data.gov.uk.

Since 2024, councils have used the service to check their data and track its quality through a dashboard. We're now making it more secure, clearer about what needs fixing, and faster at getting council data onto the platform.

Our plans can change based on what we learn from our users and from testing. We aim to update this roadmap every 3 months. You can also read the roadmap for the whole Planning Data Platform.

Last updated 18 September 2026. Next update due December 2026.

Our goal

Local planning authorities can provide good quality planning data quickly, and always know what to do next to improve it.

What this means for you

If you provide planning data for a local planning authority, you'll experience:

  • Clearer priorities: you'll see exactly where a problem is and which fixes will make the biggest difference to the quality of your data.
  • Faster publishing: your data will reach the platform more quickly, with fewer manual steps between submitting it and seeing it live.
  • Better visibility: you'll know where your submission is in the process, and we'll tell you when something changes.
  • More control: you'll be able to tell us directly when a check doesn't apply to your data, or when an endpoint should be retired.

Our progress so far

December 2025: redesigned LPA dashboard

You can now see every dataset you're responsible for in one place, including those still in development. Your dashboard shows whether data was provided by you or comes from another source, uses clearer task labels, and loads faster.

February 2026: review data from other sources

Where we hold data about your area that you didn't provide, for example data MHCLG has collected from public sources, you can now review it and download it as a CSV. This gives you a starting point for publishing your own authoritative version.

April 2026: provide local plans data

You can now check and provide your local plans data through the service. We've also improved how the service handles multiple endpoints for the same dataset, and changed "should fix" to "needs improving" to make clearer what's essential.

May 2026: check before you provide

You now check your data before you provide it, so problems are caught before your submission reaches us, not afterwards.

June 2026: match your columns to our standards

If your column names don't match the specification, you can now tell us how they map during the check. You don't need to change your source system. Tasks for your data are also now shown directly on your dashboard.

July 2026: avoid duplicate submissions

The service now tells you if you try to submit an endpoint we already have, including ones that are still being processed.

September 2026: clearer help with local plans

You now get guidance and warnings for local plan fields that only become required at certain stages of your timetable. The service also tells you when it detects more than one plan in your data.

What we're planning to do next

By December 2026: secure sign-in for your authority

You'll sign in with your council email address using a secure link sent to your inbox, so there's no password to remember. Only people from your authority will be able to provide data for it. For example, to submit Camden's brownfield land register you'll need a camden.gov.uk email address.

By December 2026: a clearer view of what needs fixing

Your dashboard will break tasks down by endpoint. If you have five brownfield land registers, you'll see which one needs attention. You'll also be able to compare the data you gave us with how it appears on the platform, so issues are easier to trace. We're redesigning the dashboard so it stays easy to use as the number of datasets grows. If you publish data from ArcGIS or a Web Feature Service (WFS), you'll also get clearer guidance on what we need and how to set up your systems.

By March 2027: more control over your tasks and endpoints

Some of our checks flag things that might be wrong rather than things that definitely are. You'll be able to tell us when there's a good reason for this and dismiss the task. You'll also be able to retire endpoints that are no longer in use, and you'll see a reminder when your brownfield land register is due for its annual review. The service will also better support authorities that provide data jointly.

By March 2027: know what's happening with your data

When you submit new data, your dashboard will show that it's waiting to be processed, so you know we've received it. We'll also email you when something changes. For example, we'll let you know if your URL stops working, or if new checks find problems that need your attention.

Later: a dashboard built around data quality

Instead of labelling your data as live, needs improving or error, your dashboard will show where each dataset sits on a data quality scale, from authoritative to usable to trustworthy. You'll see which tasks will move your data up a level, and warnings about data that may need a second look. Your checks will come directly from the national data specification, so they always reflect the latest standards.

Later: automatic publishing for clean data

If your data passes all our checks and comes from a reliable, authoritative source, it will go onto the platform automatically, without anyone on our team needing to review it first.

More information

For more information about this work, contact the Planning Data team at digitalland@communities.gov.uk.
